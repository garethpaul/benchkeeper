## Why the one-point change is warranted—and limited

The earlier impact assessment specifically deducted for JSON-only transfer and repeated intake entry. The new path accepts a bounded CSV or spreadsheet selection, validates every row, requires explicit parts status, shows a review, rejects stale confirmation and appends atomically. Native and manual-only browser flows verify that the existing nine appointments remain unchanged and the new requests appear in the shared state. One undo reverses the batch.

This demonstrates a practical connection to the registration-sheet workflow described by organizers. It does **not** show that an organizer finds it easy, that arbitrary partner exports can be imported unchanged, or that it saves a measured number of minutes. Those limitations retain three points of deduction under impact. The separate 91-point prospective design estimate remains unchanged and is not substituted for achieved evidence.

## Strongest objections to this score

- **“It is an optimizer with a tool wrapper.”** The ten tools alone would not rebut that. The strongest evidence is the shared review object, explicit consequences, live human-draft preservation and source-bound approval. The counterfactual must remain understandable in the two-minute story.
- **“Most repair cafés are walk-in.”** Correct; the scope is the smaller pre-booked/hybrid segment documented in organizer material, not all events. The product keeps walk-in reserves but is not a complete walk-in operations platform.
- **“Synthetic trials cannot prove usefulness.”** Correct. They establish implemented behavior and find defects, not demand, model reliability or repair outcomes. The 90 estimate depends on the rules' demonstrated-potential standard, not an invented field study.
- **“A runnable local app is not a valid submission yet.”** Correct. The rules also require publishing artifacts. No hosted URL, public repository or public video exists. Those remain explicit user-controlled steps, not placeholder links.
